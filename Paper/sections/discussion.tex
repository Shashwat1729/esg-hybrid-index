\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Public-Data ESG Is Mostly Financial Quality}

The construct-validity audit has a simple explanation. When direct disclosure
is missing, each proxy is chosen for a plausible link between a financial
ratio and an ESG outcome: margins and emissions \cite{konar2001does}, revenue
per employee and satisfaction \cite{edmans_2011}, cash returned and community
investment. Each link is defensible in isolation. Aggregated, however, they
turn the E and S pillars into a within-sector profitability and payout score.
That score is largely spanned by the financial factors, and it carries the
index's whole forward risk signal. The G pillar, built on third-party
governance assessments, is the only component that adds information roughly
orthogonal to the rest, yet that information did not predict risk or
returns in our window. The practical
implications are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item report provenance and redundancy per pillar, not just coverage;
\item do not describe proxy E and S pillars as environmental or social
  performance;
\item treat governance scores as the only component that currently adds
  independent information.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why In-Sample Evidence Misled}

The four artefacts in Section~\ref{sec:res_insample} share a structure: the
validation target was not independent of the thing being validated.
\begin{itemize}
\item Trailing returns overlap the price history used by the risk-adjusted
  and stability factors.
\item The ``forward'' quality proxy shares inputs with the factors, and the
  isotonic correction was fitted to it.
\item The bootstrap perturbed scores instead of data, so it measured the
  injected noise.
\end{itemize}
Every such design yields favourable numbers whether or not the index works.
This is the mechanism behind the replication failures documented for factor
research \cite{harvey_2016, bailey_2017}. The defence is procedural: freeze
the construction at a date, and test on outcomes that occur after that date.

\subsection{ESG as a Risk Tilt, and Its Price}

The forward evidence supports a risk channel, but a narrow one. Higher ESG
predicts lower volatility beyond trailing volatility and nine other
characteristics, in line with \cite{albuquerque_2019}. It does not predict
beta or drawdowns once sector and country are controlled. The decomposition
in Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanism} limits what this channel can be credited
to:
\begin{itemize}
\item the measured ESG cells carry none of the signal;
\item the proxy-heavy S pillar carries all of it;
\item recent realised volatility absorbs most of it.
\end{itemize}
The defensible reading is that public-data ESG, as built here, is a
slow-moving low-risk characteristic assembled from financial data. It is not
evidence that environmental or social performance lowers risk. The evidence
also does not support a return channel over six months. In a window in which both markets rose, the risk tilt was expensive,
because an ESG-only screen loaded on defensive sectors and avoided technology.
This is the trade-off that equilibrium models predict: sustainable
assets can serve as hedges while offering lower expected returns
\cite{pastor_2021, pedersen_2021}. A single six-month window cannot estimate
a premium. It can, however, show the size of the regime risk that an
ESG-tilted mid-cap portfolio takes on relative to its universe.

\subsection{Implications for Index Builders}

Five recommendations follow from the evidence.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Provenance per cell and redundancy per pillar} should be standard
  disclosures for any ESG index built from public data.
\item \emph{Freeze and date} the construction, and treat any number computed
  on the snapshot cross-section as descriptive.
\item \emph{Report OOS results with power.} Report the minimum detectable
  effect: with a few hundred firms, a single cross-section can reject only
  large effects.
\item \emph{Attribute signals to sources.} Before an out-of-sample effect is
  credited to ESG, re-estimate it separately on measured, proxy and imputed
  cells, and control for recent risk.
\item \emph{Generate the paper from the code.} In this project, every
  discrepancy between the manuscript and the pipeline found by the audit was
  a hand-copied or hand-written number.
\end{enumerate}
