\begin{abstract}
Open research pipelines increasingly build ESG scores for mid-cap firms from
public data. We ask what such scores measure and how they should be
validated. We build a reproducible nine-factor ESG--financial index for
\NMid US and Indian mid-caps with cell-level provenance, and audit it.

\emph{Construct validity is weak.} Only \NIndUsed of \NIndDeclared ESG
indicators carry information. Most E and S cells are financial proxies, and
the other factors explain $R^2=\RSqE$ and \RSqS of those pillars.

\emph{In-sample validation misleads.} Trailing-return ``ICs'', a
contemporaneous ``forward'' proxy and a misapplied bootstrap made the index
look strong.

\emph{Out of sample, the index does not predict returns.} In a hold-out
window (\SnapshotDate to \OOSEndDate, $N=\NOOS$) with hypotheses fixed in
advance, the composite's IC is \HOneEst (95\% CI $[\HOneLo,\HOneHi]$). The IC
is negative under \WShareNeg\% of random weightings.

\emph{ESG predicts lower volatility, but not as ESG.} Higher ESG predicts
lower volatility beyond trailing volatility (partial $\rho=\HTwoEst$, Holm
$p=\HTwoHolm$), even with \NControls risk controls and sector effects. The signal
comes from financial-proxy cells, not measured ESG data (partial
$\rho=\PartMeas$). It adds only \CVDeltaESG to out-of-fold $R^2$ and weakens
given recent volatility. Meanwhile, an ESG-only top-\TopN portfolio trailed
the universe by \ExESGFullAbs percentage points.

Public-data ESG thus acts as a slow-moving low-risk tilt, not as ESG
information or alpha. We pre-register a confirmatory second window, and one
command regenerates every number in the paper.
\end{abstract}
