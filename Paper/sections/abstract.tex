\begin{abstract}
Open research pipelines increasingly build environmental, social and
governance (ESG) scores for mid-cap firms from public data. We ask what such
scores measure and how they should be validated, using a reproducible
nine-factor ESG--financial index for \NMid US and Indian mid-caps in which
every data cell records its source. Construct validity is weak: only
\NIndUsed of \NIndDeclared ESG indicators carry information, most E and S
cells are financial proxies, and the other factors explain $R^2=\RSqE$ and
\RSqS of those two pillars. Common in-sample evidence, namely trailing-return
information coefficients (ICs), a contemporaneous ``forward'' proxy and a
misapplied bootstrap, made the index look strong. In a hold-out window
(\SnapshotDate to \OOSEndDate, $N=\NOOS$) with hypotheses fixed in advance,
the composite does not predict returns (IC $=\HOneEst$, 95\% CI
$[\HOneLo,\HOneHi]$), and the IC is negative under \WShareNeg\% of random
weightings. Higher ESG does predict lower volatility beyond trailing
volatility (partial $\rho=\HTwoEst$, Holm $p=\HTwoHolm$), and the effect
survives \NControls risk controls and sector effects. The signal comes from
the proxy-built S pillar, while measured ESG data show none of it (partial
$\rho=\PartMeas$); it adds \CVDeltaESG to out-of-fold $R^2$ and weakens given
recent volatility. An ESG-only top-\TopN portfolio trailed the universe by
\ExESGFullAbs percentage points. Public-data ESG therefore behaves as a
slow-moving low-risk tilt. We pre-register a confirmatory second window, and
a single command regenerates every number in the paper.
\end{abstract}
