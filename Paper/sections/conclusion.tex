\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper develops and evaluates a transparent, open-source, 9-factor,
202-variable ESG-integrated ranking framework for 276 mid-cap companies across
US and Indian markets. The resulting multi-factor index ranks higher than
single-factor alternatives on cross-sectional quality proxies in stressed
conditions and provides consistent ranking quality across rolling-rebalance
periods. Rolling rebalance tests indicate consistent cross-sectional
superiority relative to the benchmark proxy, with average excess momentum proxy of
$+7.38\%$. Performance remains economically meaningful in bear-market
conditions, with $+7.27$ percentage points of excess performance, bear-market excess-momentum information
ratio $=0.585$, and portfolio beta $=0.82$.

The core empirical finding is that ESG contributes mainly as a risk filter
within a broader multi-factor system, not as a standalone alpha source. Higher
ESG is associated with lower volatility ($\rho=-0.357$, $p<0.001$) and lower
beta ($\rho=-0.146$, $p=0.015$), but not with superior Sharpe outcomes:
high-ESG Q5 Sharpe is $0.145$ versus $0.458$ for low-ESG Q1. Factor ablation
shows that \texttt{growth\_score} is the most influential individual component
($\tau = 0.777$ when removed), while low multicollinearity (maximum VIF
$=2.80$) supports interpretability and indicates that the composite is not
driven by redundant signals.

The main methodological contribution is the rigor of the validation process.
Overfitting controls remain within acceptable bounds (CV ratio $=0.499$;
walk-forward ratio $=0.978$), capacity analysis indicates practical
investability from roughly \$25M to \$500M AUM with failure beyond \$1$B+, and
high-cap generalization testing demonstrates full transferability to large-cap
universes: 45 S\&P 500 companies scored using identical methodology yield
Kendall $W = 0.933$ (very strong agreement), 9/9 factors pass the z-score
stability test ($|z| < 2.5$), rank stability reaches Spearman $\rho = 0.9993$,
and only 0.2\% of factor-benchmark pairs show degradation. The Friedman test
confirms no systematic bias ($\chi^2 = 1.38$, $p = 0.50$), while score
distributions align with economic expectations (large-cap ESG mean $= 59.9$
versus mid-cap $48.4$). Unsupervised structure diagnostics remain stable
(three PCA components explain $67.4\%$ of variance; clustering silhouette
$=0.261$). At the same time, bootstrap confidence intervals remain wide
(roughly 247--255 positions), and only $32.2\%$ of names remain stable under
$\pm 20\%$ weight perturbations. Taken together, these results validate the
framework as a ranking methodology with integrated ESG risk controls, while
still requiring continued validation under fully live, time-indexed execution.

Cross-sectional market proxies remain informative but bounded in
interpretation: the observed cross-sectional superiority reflects proxy-based
ranking behavior rather than realized live-trading returns (M4 caveat).

\subsection*{Key contributions and implications}

The contributions of this study are four-fold. First, it presents a
transparent 9-factor, 202-variable ESG framework specifically designed for
mid-cap stocks. Second, it implements a hybrid ESG data pipeline with explicit
provenance tracking across Yahoo, SEC, and proxy-derived indicators. Third, it
introduces a rigorous validation suite spanning cross-validation, bootstrap
analysis, walk-forward tests, ablation studies, capacity analysis, and
external generalization checks, while also showing meaningful profile
differentiation between \texttt{esg\_first} and \texttt{fin\_first}
(Kendall $\tau=0.593$; top-10 overlap $=7/10$). Fourth, it demonstrates that
ESG integration contributes primarily through risk filtering rather than alpha
generation. The final balanced-profile top-ranked company is INCY
(Healthcare; Pref $=85.4$, ESG $=59.1$, Financial $=62.8$).

\subsection*{Future Work}

Several extensions could strengthen and generalise these findings:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Commercial ESG data integration.} Incorporate provider-grade
  datasets (e.g., Refinitiv and MSCI) to strengthen convergent and
  discriminant validity of the hybrid ESG pipeline.
  \item \textbf{Time-series implementation testing.} Extend from
  cross-sectional evaluation to event-dated, time-series backtesting with
  actual rebalance dates and realized execution assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Geographic expansion.} Test transferability in European and
  Asia-Pacific mid-cap universes to evaluate robustness across regulatory and
  disclosure regimes.
  \item \textbf{Dynamic regime-aware weighting.} Develop market-regime
  adaptive factor weighting to improve responsiveness while preserving
  transparency and overfitting discipline.
\end{enumerate}
