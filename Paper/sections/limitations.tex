\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Recent robustness upgrades have resolved or materially reduced major
technical weaknesses. Overfitting is now addressed more convincingly (CV
overfit ratio: 0.499; walk-forward ratio: 0.978). Capacity is only partially
resolved: the strategy passes for moderate mandates from roughly \$25M to
\$500M, but fails at \$1B+ under realistic mid-cap execution assumptions.
These improvements strengthen the framework, but inherent data and design
limitations remain.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Hybrid ESG construction.} ESG inputs combine real observations
and proxy-derived components. Of 276 firms, 213 have real Yahoo ESG-related
coverage, 102 have SEC-derived governance inputs, and 6 still rely primarily
on proxy construction. Large parts of the E and S pillars therefore remain
constructed rather than directly observed. Consequently, the observed
ESG--financial relationship ($R^2=0.544$) partly reflects the construction
methodology itself.

\item \textbf{Cross-sectional single-period design (M4, partially addressed).} The core study uses a
single snapshot rather than a multi-year time-series backtest; reported
``returns'' are cross-sectional momentum proxies, not forward realized
P\&L at timestamped rebalance dates. To address this, \texttt{21\_temporal\_stability.py} attempts a true
out-of-sample test by downloading realized forward returns (\texttt{T}$\rightarrow$\texttt{T+1m/3m/6m}) via
\texttt{yfinance} and correlating them with factor scores at \texttt{T}; when offline it falls back to momentum
proxies but labels the source as \texttt{proxy} vs.\ \texttt{real\_yfinance\_forward} in every output table.

\item \textbf{Zero-variance binary ESG inputs (handled, not proxied).} Several binary ESG indicators
show no cross-sectional variation in this sample
(\texttt{carbon\_reduction\_target}, \texttt{anti\_corruption\_policy},
\texttt{human\_rights\_policy}: all proportion $=1.00$). They are
automatically excluded by the zero-variance filter at scoring time and
therefore do not contribute (nor dilute) discriminative ranking information;
they are not imputed as proxies.

\item \textbf{FX conversion (proxy when offline).} INR financials are converted
using a live INR/USD rate from \texttt{INR=X} at run time when internet
is available; offline the pipeline falls back to a fixed proxy
(\$\textyen 83/\$, March 2024 RBI). Realized annual FX variation is
$\pm 5$--$8\%$; the fallback can introduce cross-country comparability
error in level-based financial metrics. The rate and its source
(\texttt{live} vs. \texttt{config\_fallback}) are recorded in
\texttt{cleaning\_metadata.json} and disclosed in every run log.

\item \textbf{Partial factor monotonicity.} Factor ranking monotonicity is
stronger against the forward-quality proxy ($7/8$ factors monotonic) than
against the JT return proxy ($2/9$ factors monotonic). The signal is therefore
economically more coherent for quality-oriented interpretation than for short
return prediction.

\item \textbf{Value factor countercyclicality (M1).} The value factor
currently shows an anti-monotonic pattern, consistent with a strong
growth-over-value regime during 2020--2024 (growth $\gg$ value). This
weakens interpretation of value as a stable cross-sectional signal in the
present sample.

\item \textbf{Individual ranking stability.} Bootstrap analysis indicates
that confidence intervals for top companies remain very wide (roughly
247--255 rank positions), effectively spanning most of the universe even
though portfolio-level ordering is highly stable (Kendall tau $= 1.000$).
This supports portfolio-level use more strongly than precise single-name rank
interpretation.

\item \textbf{ESG predicts risk more than returns.} ESG return prediction
is intentionally acknowledged as weak: ESG\_composite IC for return proxies
is low and not statistically significant (IC $=0.068$, $p=0.263$). However,
ESG retains significant risk information, with higher ESG associated with lower
volatility and lower beta. Therefore, ESG is better interpreted here as a
risk filter than a standalone return predictor.

\item \textbf{Survivorship bias risk (Mo2).} The contemporaneous universe
excludes delisted firms, which can bias cross-sectional rankings and
downside-risk estimates upward relative to a fully survivorship-free panel.

\item \textbf{Cross-validation weight mismatch.} Weight search under
cross-validation can produce market-dominant allocations
(e.g., \texttt{market\_score} near 77\%), which do not match the deployed
balanced-profile market weight of only 5\% used for final ranking
construction.

\item \textbf{ESG provenance bias risk.} The governance pillar includes
mixed-source inputs, and provenance diagnostics indicate elevated bias risk
for \texttt{G\_score} when combining Yahoo and SEC source channels.
Source-dependent measurement effects may therefore influence cross-firm
comparability.

\item \textbf{Geographic scope.} The analyzed universe (276 companies)
is restricted to the US and India. External validity for Europe and broader
Asia-Pacific markets remains untested and requires explicit expansion in
future datasets.

\item \textbf{No commercial ESG benchmark source in-sample.} The framework has
not yet been validated using commercial ESG datasets (e.g., Refinitiv, MSCI,
S\&P Global) within the full universe. This limits triangulation against
institutional-grade vendor methodologies.
\end{enumerate}
