\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

The main limitation is that the OOS test covers one window of \TradingDaysUS
trading days in a rising market. With $N=\NOOS$, only $|\rho|\ge\MDEOOS$ is
detectable at 80\% power, so H1 and H3 exclude large effects rather than
small ones, and the risk result may not generalise to other regimes. In
addition, the hypotheses and analysis code were fixed before OOS prices were
downloaded but after the calendar window had passed, the profile weights
were revised inside the window, and the authors' general knowledge of market
conditions cannot be excluded. The frozen snapshot artefacts and the full
reporting of alternative weightings limit these risks, since the
February~2026 weights and \WShareNeg\% of random weightings give the same
return conclusion. The mechanism analyses of Section~\ref{sec:res_mechanism}
are exploratory. The second window (Section~\ref{sec:prereg_design}) was
registered before it opened precisely so that H2 and H4 can be confirmed or
refuted there.

The findings concern public-data ESG as constructed here. Our E and S
pillars are not measurements of environmental or social performance, and the
results need not transfer to vendor ratings, which have their own validity
problems \cite{berg_2022, christensen_2022}. The measured cells are mostly
governance items, so the null for measured cells is not a test of measured
environmental data. The governance pillar itself depends on ISS scores
redistributed by a free data interface, which cover \NISSFirms of \NMid
firms and may not remain available. The provenance sub-scores also average
indicator ranks with equal weights, so they show where the signal lives
rather than how the composite would behave with a single data source.

Three design choices affect generality. The candidate list was taken from
index constituents at the time of collection rather than from a
point-in-time membership history, so survivorship before the evaluation
window cannot be excluded. US and Indian firms are scored against a common
reference that includes \NLarge large caps; country-neutral returns remove
market-level differences in outcomes but not in disclosure regimes, and with
$N=\NIndia$ the Indian estimates are imprecise, although they have the same
sign as the US ones (Table~\ref{tab:ext_robust}). Finally, portfolio results
are equal-weighted and single-period, with stylised round-trip costs and no
model of market impact at scale.
